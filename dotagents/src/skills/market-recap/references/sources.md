# Sources and release calendar

## Source tiers

Cite the highest tier you can reach. Mark anything below tier 1 as
"(secondary)" in the report.

| Tier | Sources | Use for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Primary | bea.gov, bls.gov, federalreserve.gov, newyorkfed.org, home.treasury.gov, census.gov, dol.gov, ismworld.org, nber.org, conference-board.org, sca.isr.umich.edu, richmondfed.org, fred.stlouisfed.org, cmegroup.com (FedWatch), cboe.com | Every economic figure and the policy rate |
| 2. Wire and major outlets | Reuters, AP, Bloomberg, CNBC, Wall Street Journal, Yahoo Finance live blogs, CNN, NBC | Market moves, movers, causes, breaking news |
| 3. Aggregators and blogs | Trading Economics, Benzinga, Investing.com, FXStreet, Substack notes, calendar sites | Leads only. Confirm against tier 1 or 2 when the figure matters |

## Pitfalls

- Live blogs update all day. Two sources can both be right at different
  times. Report the time of each figure.
- Search results can surface stale pages under today's date. Check that the
  period in the article matches the period you report.
- Some releases skip a figure. In 2026 BLS published no 12-month CPI rate for
  October because October 2025 prices were never collected during the 2025
  shutdown. Report the gap and any labeled estimates.
- A government funding lapse can freeze agency websites. Say so if a page
  shows that notice.
- Seasonal adjustment moves weekly claims a lot around holidays. Note large
  gaps between adjusted and unadjusted figures.
