# Market recap: Wednesday, November 25, 2026

**Bottom line:** Stocks fell for a second straight day on hotter inflation,
proposed new tariffs, and a continued AI and chip selloff. **The US is not in
a recession.** No core recession indicator is signaling. The live risk is
inflation that is rising while the Fed leans toward a rate hike.

Data gathered on the morning of November 25, 2026. Market figures are
intraday snapshots unless marked "close".

| Dow | | about -2.0% | Late morning, Yahoo Finance |

One report says the S&P 500 slipped back into a correction and the Nasdaq
sits 17.5% below its record.

**Why:**

1. **Hot inflation.** October core PCE rose to 3.3% y/y against 3.2%
   expected, released 8:30 a.m. ET. Traders moved toward a December Fed hike.
2. **New tariffs.** The administration proposed Section 301 tariffs of up to
   20% on China, the EU, and 12 other trading partners after an 8-month
   investigation into excess manufacturing capacity. They require hearings
   and a public comment period first, so January is the earliest start.
3. **AI and chip selloff.** Tuesday's Nvidia-led rout extended on renewed
   fears that AI stock prices are too high.

**Biggest movers:**

- Nvidia (NVDA): about -8.7%. Led the decline.
- Broadcom (AVGO): about -6.3%.
- Microsoft (MSFT): about -3.7%.
- Sandisk, Micron, Bloom Energy, Lam Research, Applied Materials: each down
  about 5.9% to 6.6% premarket, after losses Tuesday.
- Dell (DELL): fell sharply despite better-than-expected quarterly results.
- Ambarella (AMBA, not in the S&P 500): about -34% on weak guidance.
- Tuesday's S&P 500 extremes, for context: Dollar Tree +11.2%, Analog
  Devices -17.4%.

**Breaking news:**

- Nvidia: Jensen Huang was reinstated as CEO on Monday. President Trump took
  credit, said Huang should "tell the truth" to lawmakers about the scandal,
  called a pardon "an option" he does not expect to need, and said a
  government equity stake in Nvidia remains possible.
- Q3 GDP was revised up to 4.3% annualized, released 8:30 a.m. ET.
- Jobless claims fell to 189,000, tying the 2026 low.
- US markets close Thursday, November 26, for Thanksgiving and close early
  Friday, November 27.

**Verdict: No.** NBER has declared no recession, real GDP grew 4.3% in Q3,
and none of the core labor or output indicators is in recession territory.
The softest spot is real consumer spending, which was flat in October, and
industrial production, which fell 0.4% in one month. Only the NBER officially
dates US recessions, and it does so months after the fact.
